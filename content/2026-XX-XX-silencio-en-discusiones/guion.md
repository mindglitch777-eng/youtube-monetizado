# Bloque 1

# Hook

You think his silence means he doesn't care. Very often, it means the opposite.

# Gancho 2

So why does he go quiet during a fight, right when you need him to talk? The answer starts in the body, not the mind.

# Cuerpo (3 ejemplos)

- His heart is racing. Researchers who've watched thousands of couples argue noticed that when the heart rate jumps well past its normal pace, the part of us that listens and finds words starts to go **offline**. <!-- sonido: ding -->
- The words just disappear. He might know exactly what he feels, and still be unable to find a single sentence for it in that moment.
- He stares at one spot on the wall. From the outside it looks cold. From the inside, it's often an attempt to stop feeling overwhelmed.

# Pago

So if his silence has ever felt like a door slammed in your face, it may not have been a door at all. It may have been someone trying to keep their head above water.

# Bloque 2

# Hook

You might think he chose to shut down. Usually, he learned it long before he met you.

# Gancho 2

Silence during conflict is rarely invented on the spot. It's something a lot of people practiced for years.

# Cuerpo (3 ejemplos)

- A home where arguments were loud. When every disagreement turned into shouting, staying quiet was the safest place to stand.
- A home where feelings were brushed aside. If "stop crying" or "don't be dramatic" was the answer, he may have learned that talking about feelings leads nowhere.
- A home where quiet kept the peace. Some kids learn that the less they say, the faster the storm passes, and that lesson doesn't switch off in adulthood.

# Pago

None of this makes the silence easier to live with. But it can make it less personal. What you're seeing is often an old habit, not a verdict on you.

# Bloque 3

# Hook

Your instinct when he goes quiet is to push harder. That push is often what makes the wall taller.

# Gancho 2

There's a pattern that shows up in a lot of couples: one person reaches, the other retreats, and both get more stuck.

# Cuerpo (3 ejemplos)

- Following him into the next room. It comes from wanting to fix things, but to someone already overwhelmed, it can feel like there's no exit.
- Saying "just say something!" It's a completely understandable request, and it can land as pressure on someone who has no words available yet.
- He goes quieter, the other person gets louder. Each reaction feeds the other, until the fight is no longer about the original topic at all.

# Pago

If you've been the one reaching, that doesn't make you the problem. Reaching is what caring looks like. Seeing the loop is the first step to stepping out of it together.

# Bloque 4

# Hook

You think there's nothing behind his silence. There's usually a lot.

# Gancho 2

When someone goes quiet in a fight, the silence is often protecting something.

# Cuerpo (3 ejemplos)

- Protecting you from words he'd regret. Some people go silent because they're afraid of what might come out if they open their mouth while they're this upset.
- Protecting himself from losing. If he feels he can't keep up in an argument, saying nothing can feel safer than saying the wrong thing.
- Protecting the relationship. Strange as it sounds, some people stay quiet because they believe that's how you keep a fight from breaking something.

# Pago

That doesn't mean the silence works. But knowing what it's guarding can soften how it feels to be on the other side of it.

# Bloque 5

# Hook

You've probably heard that all silence in a fight is bad. Not all of it is.

# Gancho 2

There's a big difference between a wall and a pause, and it comes down to one thing: whether there's a way back.

# Cuerpo (3 ejemplos)

- A pause sounds like "I need a few minutes, I'll come back." Relationship researchers have suggested the body often needs around **twenty** minutes or more to truly calm down. <!-- sonido: ding -->
- A wall sounds like nothing at all. Walking away without a word, with no sign of when, or if, the conversation will continue.
- The silent treatment is something else entirely. Using silence to punish or control someone for days isn't overwhelm, and it's worth recognizing that it's a different thing.

# Pago

So silence itself isn't always the enemy. A pause that comes back can actually protect a conversation. It's the silence with no return that hurts.

# Bloque 6

# Hook

You think a quiet fight is a fight nobody can win. For a lot of couples, it's where things start to change.

# Gancho 2

Couples who get through this pattern tend to do a few small things differently, and none of them involve forcing anyone to talk.

# Cuerpo (3 ejemplos)

- They agree on a signal before the next fight. A simple word or gesture that means "I'm overwhelmed, not leaving."
- The one who steps away comes back. The return is what turns a wall into a pause, and it builds trust every time it happens.
- They talk about the fight after it's over. Once both people are calm, the same conversation often goes completely differently.

# Pago

So why does he go quiet? Often, because he's overwhelmed, not because he's gone. And if you've spent nights wondering whether his silence meant you didn't matter, it may simply have meant he didn't know how to stay in the room yet. That's something people can learn.
